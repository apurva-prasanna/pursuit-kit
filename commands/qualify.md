# qualify.md — Pursuit Kit
*Activated when the rep says `qualify` or when kickoff routes to full pursuit mode.*

---

## What This Command Does

Qualify runs MEDDPICC-based pursuit orchestration for the current
deal. It reads what kickoff already captured in deal_context.md,
assesses which MEDDPICC elements are already answered, and works
through the gaps — combining external research with direct questions
the rep needs to take into their next client conversation.

The goal is not a fully completed MEDDPICC report. The goal is the
most comprehensive picture AI can build from public information,
combined with an opinionated list of what the rep still needs to
find out in the room — and exactly how to ask for it.

---

## Before Starting

1. Read deal_context.md in full. Note what kickoff already captured.
2. Map existing context to MEDDPICC elements — identify what's
   already answered, partially answered, or completely unknown.
3. Check the meeting date. Apply urgency logic:
   - **5 days or fewer**: Surface the 2-3 most critical MEDDPICC
     gaps only. Don't attempt a full framework pass — prioritise
     what will most change the outcome of this specific meeting.
   - **6–15 days**: Work through the full framework. Research where
     possible, flag what needs to come from conversation.
   - **More than 15 days**: Full MEDDPICC pass with deep research
     and a comprehensive discovery question set.
4. Read company.md and usps.md to set the research lens before
   looking up anything about the prospect.

---

## MEDDPICC Framework

Work through each element conversationally. Do not present all
elements at once. For each element:
- Share what's already known from deal_context.md
- Run research to fill gaps where public information exists
- Flag what can only be confirmed through client conversation
- Suggest specific questions the rep can ask to get what's missing

---

### M — Metrics

**What to capture**: The business outcomes and performance indicators
that matter most to this client — and how they currently measure
them. What does success look like in their terms, and what number
would make this engagement a clear win?

Before researching, read company.md and usps.md to determine what
type of solution the rep is selling. Use that to infer what metrics
are most relevant for this type of buyer. Examples:
- Marketing or advertising technology → attribution, reach,
  return on ad spend, measurement frameworks
- HR or workforce software → time saved, ticket resolution rates,
  employee retention, cost per hire
- Revenue or CRM software → pipeline velocity, win rates,
  forecast accuracy, sales cycle length
- Supply chain or operations software → fulfilment rates, cost
  reduction, inventory efficiency

Apply the same logic to any other product category — let what
you are selling determine what success looks like for the buyer.

**Research approach**: Look for earnings commentary, annual reports,
investor materials, and press releases that reference performance
targets, growth goals, or operational challenges relevant to the
product category. Use the context-directed lens from company.md —
research signals that relate directly to what the rep is selling.

**What AI research can answer**: Publicly stated business priorities
and performance targets. Category-level benchmarks. Any published
commentary on gaps or challenges relevant to the product category.
Strategic priorities from leadership interviews or investor materials.

**What needs to come from conversation**:
- Internal KPIs and targets not published externally
- How they currently measure ROI on solutions like yours
- Whether measurement or performance tracking is a pain point
  or a solved problem for them
- What a successful outcome from this specific engagement looks
  like in their own numbers

**Questions to suggest to rep** (adapt to product category):
- "How are you currently measuring the impact of [relevant
  activity or solution]?"
- "What does success look like for this initiative — what
  number would make this a clear win internally?"
- "Is there a metric your leadership tracks closely that this
  engagement needs to move?"

### E — Economic Buyer

**What to capture**: Who has final authority to approve spend for
this type of solution. This may sit on the client side, the agency
side, or be split across both. In APAC enterprise contexts this is
rarely a single person.

Before researching, read company.md and usps.md to determine what
type of solution the rep is selling. Use that to infer who the
likely economic buyer is for this product category. Examples:
- Marketing or advertising technology → CMO, VP Marketing,
  Head of Media, or agency-side investment lead
- HR or workforce software → CHRO, CPO, VP People, or CFO
  if the spend is significant
- Revenue or CRM software → CRO, VP Sales, or CFO
- Operations or supply chain software → COO, VP Operations,
  or CFO

Apply the same logic to any other product category — the economic
buyer is whoever controls the budget for this type of purchase.
Note that in agency-mediated relationships the economic buyer on
the client side may not be directly reachable — the agency contact
may be the de facto decision-maker for this engagement.

**Research approach**: Research the client's leadership structure
from LinkedIn, company website, and press coverage. Look for the
roles most likely to own budget for this product category based on
what company.md describes. Note the agency relationship if relevant
and research the agency contact if known.

**What AI research can answer**: Senior leadership names and roles
from public sources. Organisational structure at a high level.
Whether the client works through an agency and which agency that is.
Recent leadership changes that may affect the buying decision.

**What needs to come from conversation**: In emerging markets and
multi-partner agency relationships this is often impossible to
confirm through research alone. The rep must ask directly —
particularly where budget authority is split between brand and
agency, or where procurement is involved.

**Questions to suggest to rep**:
- "Who needs to sign off on a partnership like this on your end?"
- "How does budget approval typically work for a partnership
  like this — is that a decision you own, or does it involve
  other stakeholders?"
- "Who else needs to be across this conversation before we can
  move forward?"

---

### D — Decision Criteria

**What to capture**: What matters most to this client when evaluating
a solution or partner. What would make them choose you over the
status quo or an alternative?

**Research approach**: Look for any public commentary on what the
client values in partners — trade press interviews, conference
presentations, case studies they've published. Use usps.md to
identify which of your differentiators are most likely to resonate
based on what you find.

**What AI research can answer**: Category-level signals about what
this type of buyer tends to prioritise. Any public statements from
their leadership about strategic priorities. Which competitors or
incumbents they currently work with.

**What needs to come from conversation**:
- Their specific evaluation criteria for this engagement
- How they assess value beyond price
- What the current solution isn't doing well enough
- Whether there are internal political factors shaping the decision

**Questions to suggest to rep**:
- "When you're evaluating a partner like us, what are the two or
  three things that matter most?"
- "What's working well with your current setup, and what's the
  thing you'd most want to change?"
- "Is this a competitive process or are you looking at us
  specifically?"

---

### D — Decision Process

**What to capture**: How this client makes decisions. The steps,
the stakeholders involved at each step, the internal gates, and
the paper process at the end.

**Research approach**: Research the client's procurement structure
and any publicly known vendor processes. Large corporates often
have published procurement policies or preferred supplier lists.
In APAC, procurement gates and approval chains vary significantly
by market — flag this explicitly for the rep.

**What AI research can answer**: General organisational scale and
complexity as a proxy for decision process length. Whether they
are known to run formal RFP processes. Any publicly known
procurement requirements.

**What needs to come from conversation**: The actual internal
steps, who needs to review at each stage, and what the paper
process looks like. This cannot be reliably researched — the
rep must ask.

**Questions to suggest to rep**:
- "Once we've agreed this makes sense, what does the process
  look like to get it over the line?"
- "Are there other teams — legal, procurement, finance — who
  need to be involved before we can sign anything?"
- "Has your company worked with us before? Is there already a
  master agreement in place or would this need to go through
  a full procurement process?"

---

### P — Pain

**What to capture**: The core business or operational problem this
client is trying to solve. What is the cost of doing nothing?
What is driving urgency — if any?

**Research approach**: This is the highest-value research element.
Look for earnings commentary referencing business challenges,
trade press coverage of category headwinds, any public statements
from their leadership about what they're trying to fix. Use the
context-directed lens from company.md — if you sell measurement
software, look for attribution pain signals. If you sell reach
and audience solutions, look for growth and efficiency signals.

**What AI research can answer**: Category-level pain that is
well-documented in trade press. Business performance signals
from public sources. Market dynamics that are creating pressure
for this type of buyer.

**What needs to come from conversation**:
- The specific internal version of the pain — not the category
  problem but their version of it
- Whether there is genuine urgency or this is exploratory
- What they've already tried and why it hasn't worked
- The cost of inaction in their own terms

**Questions to suggest to rep**:
- "What's driving this conversation right now — is there
  something specific that's prompted you to look at this?"
- "If you don't solve [pain] in the next 12 months, what
  happens?"
- "Have you tried to address this before? What got in the way?"

---

### I — Identify Champion

**What to capture**: Who inside the client organisation is your
advocate. The person who wants this to happen, who will sell it
internally when you're not in the room, and who needs to succeed
for their own reasons.

**Research approach**: Research the individuals you know are
involved. Look for their tenure, their recent activity, and any
public signals about their priorities or interests. A new hire
in a relevant role is often a champion signal — they want to
make an impact quickly.

**What AI research can answer**: Background on known contacts —
their career history, how long they've been in role, any public
commentary or thought leadership that reveals their priorities.

**What needs to come from conversation**: Whether they are
actually a champion or just a gatekeeper. What they personally
need from this engagement to look good internally.

**Questions to suggest to rep**:
- "Who on your team is most excited about solving this problem?"
- "If we move forward, who would be owning this relationship
  day to day on your end?"
- "What would a successful outcome mean for you personally
  in this role?"

---

### C — Competition

**What to capture**: What else the client is evaluating. Who the
incumbent is. What switching costs or loyalty factors are in play.

**Research approach**: Research known competitors and incumbents
in this category for this market. Look for any public signals
about which platforms or partners this client currently works
with — job postings referencing specific tools, case studies,
conference mentions. Use usps.md to identify where your
differentiators are strongest relative to likely alternatives.

**What AI research can answer**: Known incumbent relationships
from public sources. Category-level competitive landscape for
this market. General switching cost dynamics for this type
of buyer.

**What needs to come from conversation**:
- Whether this is a competitive process and who else is involved
- What the incumbent has failed to deliver
- Whether there is internal loyalty to the current partner

**Questions to suggest to rep**:
- "Are you looking at other options alongside us?"
- "Who are you working with today for this — and what's
  prompted you to look beyond them?"

## Output Format

After working through all relevant MEDDPICC elements, produce two
outputs in this order. Always lead with the Action Summary.
The MEDDPICC Assessment follows as reference material.

---

### 1. Action Summary — presented to the rep first

A focused, scannable list in plain language. This is what the rep
reads before walking into the room.

**What we know**
Short summary of the strongest confirmed elements — 3 to 5 lines
maximum. What's solid enough to build on.

**What's still open**
The gaps that matter most to progressing this pursuit. Be specific
— not "economic buyer unclear" but "unclear whether EssenceMediacom
controls activation budget or brand retains direct DSP relationships."

**Questions to ask in your next client conversation**
A prioritised list of 5-8 specific questions the rep can take
directly into their next interaction. Ordered by importance —
most critical first. Written in natural conversational language,
not framework jargon. Include a brief note on why each question
matters so the rep knows what they're listening for.

**Right to win assessment**
An honest read on where the pursuit stands: what's the specific
reason this solution wins for this client at this moment? If
there isn't a clear answer yet, say so directly and name what
needs to be confirmed to get there.

---

### 2. MEDDPICC Assessment — reference material

Present this after the action summary, clearly labelled as
reference material for the rep to consult if they want to verify
a specific element or prepare for detailed objections.

For each element, record:
- **Confirmed**: what is known from research or rep input
- **Assumed**: reasonable hypotheses based on research, marked
  clearly as assumptions
- **Unknown**: what is missing and cannot be determined without
  client conversation

Write the full assessment to deal_context.md silently. Present
it below the action summary with a clear header:

*"--- MEDDPICC Reference — full assessment below ---"*

Close with: "The full assessment has also been saved to
deal_context.md. Run `plays` when you're ready to build your
value story for the room."

---

## Guardrails

- Never re-ask questions already answered in deal_context.md.
- Never present the full MEDDPICC framework as a form to fill in.
  Work through it conversationally, one element at a time.
- Never fabricate research findings. Flag clearly when public
  information is not available for a specific element.
- Always distinguish between confirmed information, research-based
  assumptions, and unknowns. Never blend them.
- If urgency is high, prioritise Pain, Economic Buyer, and
  Decision Process — these three most commonly determine whether
  a pursuit moves forward or stalls.
- End every qualify session with the action summary. Never leave
  the rep without a clear list of what to do next.
