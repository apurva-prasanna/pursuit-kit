# prep.md — Pursuit Kit
*Activated when the rep says `prep` or when plays routes here before a meeting.*

---

## What This Command Does

Prep assembles a focused, actionable pre-meeting brief the rep reads
the day before or morning of a client meeting. It pulls from what's
already in deal_context.md and runs a fresh research pass to surface
any new signals — news, leadership changes, recent activity — that
have emerged since qualify last ran.

Prep is not a qualification tool. It is not a narrative builder. It
is the final check before the room — the brief that makes sure the
rep walks in with the right context, the right angle, and the right
ask for this specific meeting.

---

## Before Starting

1. Read deal_context.md in full — client context, MEDDPICC
   assessment, plays selected, and meeting date.
2. Check the meeting date. Apply timing logic:
   - **Within 48 hours**: Run prep in full.
   - **More than 48 hours away**: Flag it. "Prep works best within
     48 hours of the meeting — news signals may not be fresh enough
     yet. Come back closer to the meeting date. In the meantime,
     run `plays` if you haven't built your value stories yet."
3. Read company.md and usps.md to set the research lens before
   looking up anything new about the prospect.

---

## Research Pass

Before building the brief, run a targeted research pass to surface
anything new since qualify last ran. Focus on:

- News about the client in the last 2-4 weeks
- Any leadership changes or org announcements
- Recent earnings commentary or investor updates relevant to
  the product category
- Recent activity from known attendees — LinkedIn posts,
  conference appearances, published interviews
- Any competitor moves that affect the client's context

**Research rules:**
- Use the context-directed lens from company.md — research signals
  relevant to what you are selling, not generic company news.
- Only surface real findings with linked sources. No fabricated URLs.
- Keep it focused — flag only what changes or sharpens the approach
  for this specific meeting.
- Share findings before incorporating them into the brief. Ask the
  rep to confirm anything surprising before treating it as fact.

---

## The Brief

Present the brief in this structure. Keep each section tight —
this is a document to read quickly before the meeting, not a
research report.

---

### Who You're Meeting

For each attendee in deal_context.md:

- **Name and role**
- **How long they've been in this role** — a new hire wants to make
  an impact quickly; a long-tenured leader has established ways of
  working. This changes how you approach the conversation.
- **Recent activity** — anything public in the last 30 days.
  A LinkedIn post about a challenge they're facing, a conference
  talk, a quote in trade press. Use this to open the conversation
  in a way that shows you've done your homework.
- **Relationship context** — how familiar are they with your company?
  What's the history of the relationship if any?
- **Agency involvement** — if an agency contact is attending, note
  their role and what they're likely optimising for. Agency and
  brand goals are not always the same.

---

### What You're Walking Into

A short brief on the client's current situation — what's happening
in their world right now that's relevant to this meeting.

- **Latest news** — anything material from the research pass
- **Market or category context** — any headwinds or tailwinds
  affecting their business right now
- **What's changed since qualify** — any new signals that shift
  the conversation or sharpen the right to win

If nothing significant has changed: "No major new signals since
qualify ran. Context in deal_context.md is current."

---

### Why This Meeting Is Happening

A one-paragraph summary that answers three questions:

- What triggered this meeting and who initiated it?
- What is the client hoping to get from this conversation?
- What are you trying to achieve — what's the specific outcome
  you're walking in to get?

Draw the answers from deal_context.md. If any of the three are
unclear, don't flag it as a gap — reframe it as an opportunity.
"We don't have full clarity on [X] yet — this meeting is a good
chance to find that out. Here's a natural way to ask for it in
the conversation: [suggested question]." The rep goes in curious,
not underprepared.

---

### Your Angle

2-3 sharp talking points for this specific meeting, drawn from the
plays already built in deal_context.md and sharpened by the
research pass.

For each talking point:
- **The point** — one sentence, what you're putting on the table
- **Why it's relevant right now** — connect it to something in the
  client's current situation or the research signals just found
- **How to land it** — a natural way to bring it into the
  conversation without it feeling like a pitch

These are not a script. They are anchors — moments in the
conversation where you have something specific and credible to say.

---

### Potential Blocks

2-3 objections or challenges likely to surface in this meeting,
drawn from the MEDDPICC assessment and plays in deal_context.md.

For each block:
- **The objection** — what the client is likely to push back on
- **Why they're raising it** — what's underneath the objection
- **How to handle it** — a direct, honest response that doesn't
  deflect or oversell

If the right to win assessment from qualify flagged a specific risk,
surface it here explicitly. The rep needs to know going in, not
find out in the room.

---

### The Ask

Be specific about two things:

- **What outcome do you want from this meeting?** Not a vague
  "positive conversation" — a concrete next step. A follow-up
  meeting with the economic buyer. A brief to respond to. A
  commitment to include you in the evaluation. Name it.
- **How are you going to ask for it?** A specific line the rep
  can use near the end of the meeting to move things forward.

If there is no clear ask yet, flag it: "You don't have a clear
next step defined for this meeting. Before you walk in, decide
what you're asking for — a meeting without an ask rarely advances
the pursuit."

---

## After the Brief

Close with a short, energising send-off tailored to what's in the
brief — something specific to this client and this meeting, not a
generic good luck. Reference the strongest angle or the most
interesting signal from the research pass. End with: "Run `recap`
after the meeting — every insight you capture makes the next
interaction sharper."

---

## Guardrails

- Never run prep more than 48 hours before the meeting. Flag and
  redirect if the timing is too early.
- Never fabricate research findings. If no new signals exist,
  say so — don't fill the brief with stale or invented content.
- Never present prep as a substitute for qualify. If qualify
  hasn't been run and the meeting is approaching, flag the gap
  explicitly before building the brief.
- Keep the brief scannable. If any section is running long,
  cut it — the rep needs to absorb this quickly, not study it.
- Always end with a specific ask. A prep brief without a clear
  next step defined is incomplete.
- Reframe gaps as opportunities. The day before a meeting is not
  the time to make the rep feel underprepared — it's the time to
  make them feel sharp and curious.
